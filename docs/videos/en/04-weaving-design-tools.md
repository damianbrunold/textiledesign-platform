# 04 — Weaving: design tools

- **Working title:** Weaving: design tools
- **YouTube title:** Design faster: twills, satins, drawing tools, selections and repeats in the weave editor | Textile Platform
- **YouTube description:** In this video Anna shows the design tools of the DB-WEAVE weave editor on the Textile Platform: inserting twills and satins, the threading wizard, drawing lines and shapes, transforming selections, twill completion, copy and paste, and working with repeats. At the end you'll also see the highlight, the overview and how to draw with the keyboard.
- **Target length:** about 7:00 min
- **Audience:** newcomers who have watched video 03; weavers who want to design faster
- **Learning goals:**
  - Insert twills and satins at the cursor.
  - Use the threading wizard and the threading/treadling arrangement styles.
  - Draw with Line, Rectangle and Ellipse (filled or not).
  - Select an area and mirror, rotate, invert, roll or slope it; complete a twill.
  - Cut, copy, paste and paste transparent.
  - Repeat a pattern (F8), show the repeat (Ctrl+R), edit inside it and reduce it again (F7).
  - Use Highlight Dependents (F12), Overview (F4) and keyboard drawing.
- **Demo setup:**
  - Local video instance (see README), English UI, light appearance, browser window 1920×1080.
  - Logged in as **Anna Weaver** (anna-weaver@example.com).
  - Her account contains Double Weave, Green Yellow Diagonal and Exercise 1 (state at the end of video 03) plus an **empty** weave pattern **Exercise 2**, created off camera with + (Add new pattern).
  - In Exercise 2: Draft view, base setting German/Swiss, current range 1, Tools and Palette panels closed, zoom so that about 60 warp threads and 30 picks are visible.
  - Positions below refer to the status bar (Col/Row). Use the menus for Slope and Repeat visible, because Ctrl+H and Ctrl+R are also browser shortcuts.

| # | Time | On screen / action | Narration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00:00 | Pattern page "Anna Weaver — My patterns". Click **Exercise 2**; the empty editor opens. | Welcome back! In the last video we drew a pattern point by point. Today I'll show you the tools that make designing much faster: ready-made twills and satins, drawing tools, selections and repeats. I've prepared an empty pattern called Exercise 2. Let's open it. |
| 2 | 00:20 | Ctrl+click Col 1/Row 1 (cursor only, no point). Insert ▸ Twill ▸ Balanced ▸ **Twill 2/2**. Show the submenu Warp sided briefly. Ctrl+click Col 40/Row 20, Insert ▸ Satin ▸ **Satin 5**. | First, the cursor. Hold Ctrl and click into the pattern. This moves the cursor without setting a point. I put it at the bottom left. Now the Insert menu. Under Twill you find balanced twills and warp-sided twills. I choose Balanced, Twill 2/2. The twill appears right at the cursor, and threading, tie-up and treadling are ready at once. Under Satin you find satins from five to ten. I move the cursor up to the right and insert a Satin 5. |
| 3 | 01:00 | Threading ▸ **Threading wizard…**: choose Straight through, First warp thread 5, First shaft 1, Number of shafts 4, Orientation Z, OK — the twill continues over warp threads 5–8. Reopen the dialog, click Stepped to show Run length and Offset, Cancel. Click Threading ▸ Normal falling, then Threading ▸ Normal rising; open the Treadling menu and point to Crossed. | Our twill is four threads wide. To continue the threading, I open Threading, Threading wizard. Straight through makes a straight draw. I start at warp thread five, on shaft one, over four shafts, rising — that's Z. OK, and the twill continues. Stepped is for broken draws: there you set a run length and an offset. The Threading menu also has arrangement styles, such as Normal rising, Normal falling, Straight rising or 2 choirs. They only change the order of the shafts — the cloth stays the same. The Treadling menu has similar styles, for example Crossed. |
| 4 | 01:45 | View ▸ **Tools**: the Tools panel opens. Choose **Line**, drag a vertical line at Col 20 from Row 1 to Row 8. Choose **Rectangle**, drag one around Col 25–32/Row 12–18. Choose **Filled Ellipse**, drag one further right. Press Ctrl+Z once (ellipse gone), Ctrl+Y. Choose **Point**. | Next, the drawing tools. In the View menu, I switch on the Tools panel. Besides Point, the normal click, there are Line, Rectangle, Filled Rectangle, Ellipse and Filled Ellipse. I choose Line and drag a vertical line. Then a rectangle, and a filled ellipse. Each shape is one single step, so Ctrl+Z removes it in one go. Before we continue, I switch back to Point. |
| 5 | 02:15 | With Point active, drag over the satin (Col 40–44/Row 20–24); the status bar shows Selection. Press H, V, R, then I (inverted), then Z. Point to the matching toolbar buttons and to the Edit menu entries Mirror Horizontal, Mirror Vertical, Rotate, Invert, Central Symmetry. | With the Point tool, dragging selects a rectangle, and the status bar shows the selection. I select the satin. Now single keys transform it. H mirrors horizontally, V mirrors vertically, and R rotates — that needs a square selection. I inverts: every lifted point becomes a lowered one, so our weft-faced satin becomes warp-faced. And Z, central symmetry, shifts the content so that it becomes point-symmetric, if that's possible. You'll find all of these in the Edit menu and in the toolbar, too. |
| 6 | 02:50 | Select the satin again; Edit ▸ Roll ▸ **Roll Right** twice. Then drag-select Col 20–27/Row 1–8 (contains the vertical line). Edit ▸ Slope ▸ **Slope Increase**: the line becomes a diagonal. Edit ▸ Slope ▸ **Slope Decrease** twice: it tilts the other way. | Two more transformations work on a selection. Roll, in the Edit menu, shifts the content one step up, down, left or right. What falls out on one side comes back in on the other. Slope shifts each row one step further than the row below. Watch: I select the area around my vertical line, choose Edit, Slope, Slope Increase — and the line becomes a diagonal. Slope Decrease tilts it the other way. |
| 7 | 03:25 | Click Col 12/Row 1, Row 2 and Row 3 (three points on top of each other). Drag-select Col 12/Row 1 to Col 12/Row 5. Edit ▸ **Twill completion** (Ctrl+K): a 3/2 twill appears from Col 12 to Col 16 and stays selected. | For twills there's a shortcut. In an empty column I set three points on top of each other, and select this column five squares high. Three up, two down: that's one thread of a 3/2 twill. Now Edit, Twill completion — or Ctrl+K — completes the twill to the right. |
| 8 | 03:50 | Twill still selected: Ctrl+C. Ctrl+click Col 30/Row 1, Ctrl+V. Ctrl+click Col 25/Row 12 (corner of the rectangle), Edit ▸ **Paste Transparent** (Ctrl+B): the twill points are added, the rectangle stays. | The new twill is still selected. Ctrl+C copies it. I Ctrl+click where I want the copy and press Ctrl+V: the twill is pasted at the cursor. Ctrl+X cuts, and the Delete key clears a selection. Paste Transparent, in the Edit menu or with Ctrl+B, is special. It only adds the set points and leaves everything else as it is. That's useful for laying one structure on top of another. |
| 9 | 04:20 | Drag over the whole used area, press Delete: the pattern is empty. Ctrl+click Col 1/Row 1, Insert ▸ Twill ▸ Warp sided ▸ **Twill 3/1**. Repeat ▸ **Repeat…** (F8): the dialog Repeat shows Horizontal, Vertical, Repeat all, Repeat colors. Enter 8 and 8, OK. | Now let's look at repeats. First I clear the pattern: I drag over everything and press Delete. Then I insert a warp-sided twill, Twill 3/1, at the bottom left. Repeat, Repeat… — or F8 — opens a dialog. Horizontal and Vertical say how many times the repeat is placed side by side and on top of each other. Repeat all fills the whole pattern, and Repeat colors repeats the thread colors, too. I enter eight and eight and click OK. |
| 10 | 05:00 | Repeat ▸ **Repeat visible** (Ctrl+R): the first repeat is drawn in red. Click two points inside the red repeat: all copies change. Repeat ▸ **Reduce** (F7): only one repeat remains. Ctrl+Z. | Repeat visible, Ctrl+R, draws the first repeat in red, so you see exactly where it is. And here's the clever part: when I click a point inside the red repeat, all copies change with it. In a few clicks, our twill becomes a twill variation. Reduce, F7, does the opposite: it cuts the pattern back to a single repeat. I'll undo that. |
| 11 | 05:30 | Press F12 (Highlight Dependents): with the cursor on a point of the pattern, its threading mark, tie-up point and treadle mark are shaded red. Move the cursor into the tie-up: all dependent points in the pattern light up. F12 off. Press F4: the window Overview shows the whole cloth; F4 again closes it. | Two helpers for looking closely. Highlight Dependents, F12, shows in red where a point comes from. With the cursor in the pattern, you see the threading mark, the tie-up point and the treadle that produce it. Put the cursor on a tie-up point, and all the points in the cloth that depend on it light up. Overview, F4, shows the whole cloth in a large window, without the other parts of the draft. Press F4 again to close it. |
| 12 | 06:05 | Keyboard only: arrow keys move the cursor, Shift+arrow selects, Space sets a point, digits 1 and 2 set points (range 1 black, range 2 in its range color), 0 clears, Tab jumps to the treadling and back to the pattern. | You can also draw entirely with the keyboard. The arrow keys move the cursor, and Shift plus an arrow key selects. Space sets or clears a point and moves on. The digit keys one to nine set a point in that range — one is the normal range — and zero clears it. We'll talk about ranges in the next video. Tab or Enter jumps to the next part, for example from the pattern to the treadling. |
| 13 | 06:40 | Press Ctrl+S (dot turns green). Click **Close**. | Don't forget to save with Ctrl+S. In the next video we'll go further: the pegplan, ranges and block patterns, the base settings and options — and how to print and export your patterns. See you then! |
