# 05 — Weaving: advanced, print and export

- **Working title:** Weaving: advanced, print and export
- **YouTube title:** Pegplan, ranges, block patterns, options, printing and export in the weave editor | Textile Platform
- **YouTube description:** The third weaving video on the Textile Platform goes deeper: ranges and block patterns, the pegplan for dobby looms, lancee, the German/Swiss, American and Scandinavian base settings, options and the weft/warp ratio. Anna then shows pattern information, printing, exporting as PNG, JPEG, SVG or PDF, and downloading the .dbw file for the DB-WEAVE desktop program.
- **Target length:** about 7:30 min
- **Audience:** weavers who know the basics from videos 03 and 04
- **Learning goals:**
  - Draw in ranges 1–9 and know the special ranges Lift out, Binding and Unbinding.
  - Turn a profile draft into a full draft with Block patterns…; know what Range patterns… does.
  - Switch to the pegplan (F9) and back; use warp lancee.
  - Choose a base setting and adjust Options for this pattern or globally; set the weft/warp ratio.
  - Create a color blending in the palette.
  - Use File ▸ Information…, Properties… and Page setup…; print, print part and export.
  - Download the .dbw file for the desktop program.
- **Demo setup:**
  - Local video instance (see README), English UI, light appearance, browser window 1920×1080.
  - Logged in as **Anna Weaver** (anna-weaver@example.com).
  - Her account contains the starter **Double Weave** (unchanged, Draft view, base setting German/Swiss), plus Green Yellow Diagonal, Exercise 1 and Exercise 2 from the earlier videos. No cloned example patterns are needed.
  - Prepared off camera: a new weave pattern **Block Exercise** containing a two-block profile drawn in range 1: points at Col 1–2/Row 1–2 and Col 3–4/Row 3–4 (eight points). Its Block patterns slots are empty. Saved, Draft view, zoomed in so the profile is large.
  - Ranges and Palette panels closed. The palette entries from index 200 onwards are not used by Double Weave (the color blending in scene 9 writes there).
  - Have a PDF viewer ready for the print output; downloads go to the default download folder.

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 00:00 | Editor with **Block Exercise** open: the small two-block profile. | Welcome to the third part of our weaving series. Today we look at the advanced features: ranges, block patterns, the pegplan, and the settings that change how your draft looks. At the end I'll show you how to print and export your patterns. I've prepared a small pattern called Block Exercise: a simple profile with two blocks. |
| 2 | 00:25 | View ▸ **Ranges**: the Ranges panel opens. Press Shift+2 (status bar shows Range 2), click an empty square: the point is drawn in the color of range 2. Open Ranges ▸ Current range and point to Range 1–9, Lift out, Binding, Unbinding. Ctrl+Z. | Every point in the pattern belongs to a range. Normally that's range one. In the View menu I open the Ranges panel. There are ranges one to nine, each with its own color. I choose range two — or press Shift+2 — and click a point. It's drawn in the color of range two. If you select an area first and then choose a range, all points in it move to that range. Besides ranges one to nine there are three special ones: Lift out, Binding and Unbinding. They mark special points in the cloth and have their own symbols, which you set in the options. I'll undo my change. |
| 3 | 01:15 | Ranges ▸ **Block patterns…**: the dialog "Substitute with block patterns" opens. Click slot **1**, choose **Twill 3/1** in the drop-down "Insert binding…". Click slot **B** (Base pattern), choose **Twill 3/1**, click **Invert**. Click **Apply**: the profile becomes a 16×16 draft with expanded threading and treadling. Point to Ranges ▸ Range patterns…. | Ranges are the key to block and range patterns. Ranges, Block patterns opens an editor with a base pattern, B, and nine further patterns — one for each range. In block mode, every square of the profile is replaced by a whole block. I select pattern one and choose Twill 3/1 under Insert binding. For the base pattern I take the same twill and click Invert. Apply — and the little profile becomes a full draft, with threading and treadling expanded, too. Range patterns works in a similar way, but keeps the size. Each point is replaced in place by the pattern of its range. That's how you fill different areas of a motif with different weaves. |
| 4 | 02:10 | Press **F9** (Extras ▸ Pegplan is checked): tie-up and treadling are replaced by the pegplan. Open the new Pegplan menu: Invert, Mirror, Delete. Click a square in the pegplan. Ctrl+Z. Press F9 again. | Many looms, especially dobby looms, lift each shaft on its own, without a tie-up. For those you need a pegplan. Press F9, or choose Extras, Pegplan. Tie-up and treadling are replaced by the pegplan. Each row is a pick, and the marks show which shafts are lifted. A new Pegplan menu appears with Invert, Mirror and Delete, and you can draw in the pegplan just like in the treadling. F9 switches back, and the editor calculates tie-up and treadling from the cloth again. |
| 5 | 02:50 | Edit ▸ Lancee ▸ **Warp lancee…**: dialog Thread ratio, "Enter the thread ratio:", enter 1 : 1, OK — an empty warp thread is inserted after every warp thread. Ctrl+Z. Ctrl+S. | Lancee helps when you want to add an extra warp or weft. Edit, Lancee, Warp lancee asks for a thread ratio. I enter one to one. After each warp thread, an empty one is inserted — room for an additional effect warp. Weft lancee does the same for the picks. I'll undo this and save. |
| 6 | 03:15 | Click **Close**, open **Double Weave**. Extras ▸ Base settings ▸ **American**, then **Scandinavian** (threading moves below the pattern), then **German/Swiss**. | Now I switch to Double Weave. Drafts are drawn differently from country to country. Under Extras, Base settings, you'll find three conventions. American uses filled squares and works from right to left. Scandinavian also puts the threading below the pattern, works from top to bottom and uses a sinking shed. German/Swiss is the default, with lines in the threading, crosses in the tie-up and dots in the treadling. Your choice is also remembered for new patterns. |
| 7 | 03:50 | Extras ▸ Options ▸ **For this pattern…**: dialog "Options (for this pattern)". Show the tabs Sizes, View, Symbols, Settings, Grid. On Symbols set Threading: to **Number**, OK — the threading shows shaft numbers. Open Extras ▸ Options ▸ **Global…** briefly, Cancel. | For finer control, open Extras, Options, For this pattern. Under Sizes you set the number of shafts, treadles, warp and weft threads, and how many shafts and treadles are visible. View sets the working directions. Under Symbols you choose the symbol for each part — I'll show the threading as numbers. Settings switches between single and multi treadle, and between rising and sinking shed. Grid sets how often a stronger grid line is drawn. Global has the same tabs, but also saves the values as your defaults. |
| 8 | 04:30 | Extras ▸ **Weft/warp ratio…**: Warp factor 1, Weft factor 1.5, OK — the rows become taller. Ctrl+Z. | Real fabric is rarely square. If your weft is thicker than the warp, you get fewer picks per centimetre. Extras, Weft/warp ratio lets you enter a warp factor and a weft factor. With a weft factor of one point five, the rows get taller, and the screen shows the proportions of the real cloth. I'll undo that. |
| 9 | 04:55 | Colors ▸ **Color blending…**: dialog "Create color blending". Click Start color ▸ From palette… (dark blue), End color ▸ From palette… (light yellow), Steps 10, Palette index 200, OK. Ctrl+F: the new gradient is visible in the palette. | For smooth color gradients, use Colors, Color blending. Choose a start color and an end color, the number of steps, and the palette index where the new colors go. Weighting moves the middle of the gradient towards one end. Click OK, and the gradient appears in the palette, ready for color stripes in warp or weft. |
| 10 | 05:25 | File ▸ **Information…**: dialog "Pattern information". Click through the tabs General, Colors, Floats, Heddles. Point to **Save…**. Close the dialog. | File, Information gives you facts about your pattern. General shows the size, the repeat, the number of shafts and treadles, and whether the cloth is warp-sided or weft-sided. Colors counts the colors in warp and weft. Floats shows the longest floats — important for a stable fabric. Heddles shows how many heddles each shaft needs. With Save you get all this as a text file. |
| 11 | 05:55 | File ▸ **Properties…**: fill in Author: "Anna Weaver", Organization: "Weaving Guild Sampleton", Notes: "Course example", OK. File ▸ **Page setup…**: show Header: and Footer: with the tokens line, OK. | Under File, Properties, you enter the author, the organization and notes. They appear in the pattern information and can be printed. Page setup defines a header and footer for printing. Placeholders such as &Pattern, &Author or &Page are filled in automatically. |
| 12 | 06:15 | Press **Ctrl+P**: a PDF is downloaded; open it in the PDF viewer and scroll through the pages. Back in the editor, File ▸ **Print part…** (Ctrl+L): dialog with Warp range, Weft range, Harness range, Treadle range, each From/To. Cancel. | To print, press Ctrl+P or choose File, Print. The platform creates a PDF with all pages, which you can print or keep. Unsaved changes are included. If you only need part of a large pattern, use Print part, Ctrl+L. There you enter the warp range, weft range, harness range and treadle range, each with From and To. |
| 13 | 06:40 | File ▸ Export ▸ **PNG image…**: the PNG is downloaded; show it briefly. Point to JPEG image…, SVG file…, PDF file…. | For pictures, use File, Export. You can choose a PNG or JPEG image, an SVG file for graphics programs, or a PDF file. The export shows the complete draft with threading, tie-up and treadling — handy for course handouts or a website. |
| 14 | 07:05 | Click **Close**, in the dialog "Close pattern" click **Don't save**. On the pattern page, click ⤓ **Download** in the row of Double Weave: the .dbw file is downloaded. | Finally, back to the pattern list. I close Double Weave without saving, because I only wanted to show you the settings. Next to each pattern there's a Download button. It gives you the .dbw file, which opens directly in the free DB-WEAVE desktop program — for example to control your loom, which only the desktop version can do. That's the end of our weaving series.
